Grape Guardians \- Client Outline & Pitch  
**Problem statement:**  
The problem we are trying to address is that SLFs tend to land on grapes during their life cycle. This is backed by the fact that there’s an average of 9.3 SLF per vine when looking at vineyards in areas infested with SLFs \[1\]. Additionally, a 2025 study estimates that in three years, there will be an 8% decrease in yield and a 10% decrease in price of grapes due to a drop in quality \[2\]. Both of these statistics are major issues, as there are no current ways to separate SLFs from harvested grapes, so we want to prevent the SLFs from getting to the vines in the first place.    
**Why it matters:** 

* Crucial to limit impacts of SLFs on the grape industry \- SLFs harm profit and economic viability  
* SLFs live longer when eating grapes \- preventing access could help to inhibit the spread of SLFs

**Proposed Directions:**  
**Concept A: Light trap**   
**What it is:** Large lights at the edges of grape fields to attract SLFs away from the grapes. On the light, there would be a trap to prevent the SLFs from leaving, such as a chamber with a one-way door.  
**How it would be used:** 

- Place lights around the grape field and make sure the trap is activated   
- When the trap is full, empty it and continue to use

**Why it's better than the status quo:**

- **Reduces chemical use:** Unlike insecticide spraying, the light trap doesn’t use chemicals, protecting grape quality, beneficial insects, and the surrounding environment.  
- **Draws SLF away from vines:** By attracting SLF to perimeter lights, it protects grape plants directly.

**End-of-semester proof-of-concept goal:** 

- A proof-of-concept prototype that can show the full function of the light-trapping mechanism.

**Risks:** 

- SLFs may be able to leave the trap or avoid entering it. (could be tested in controlled conditions)  
- Lights may not be effective during daylight. (test for SLF capture count during the day vs night)  
- Enough SLFs don’t go to the lights and the issue persists

**Concept B (primary): vineyard net**  
**What it is:** A large net to encapsulate vineyards. This is to prevent SLFs from getting to grapes.  
**How it would be used:**

- A metal structure supports a net encapsulating an entire vineyard with access gates for tractors

**Why it's better than the status quo:**

- This makes it impossible for SLFs to get onto grapevines, and therefore get caught in the harvest  
- Simple solution that doesn’t involve tinkering with complex parameters of harvesters

**End-of-semester proof-of-concept:**

- A proof-of-concept prototype that can show the structure to hold the net and articulating tractor gates

**Risks:**

- Weight of the net may require a complex structure or many supports. (test with light netting)  
- Net may block sunlight, affecting quality/quantity of the grapes. (test light levels with translucent net)  
- Farm equipment may struggle getting in and out of the enclosure. (iterate prototypes of tractor gates)

**Questions:** 

- What kinds of lights attract SLFs the best (i.e. the most effective lumen or wavelength range)? Affects the kind of light we would use for the light trap.  
- Do SLFs tend to only fly near light sources or land on them directly? Affects the range of our trap.  
- Are there animals that need to access the grapevines for them to grow properly? Affects if we move forward with the net concept \- we don’t want to hinder grape growth.  
- A study found grape sugar content decreases by 0.5° Brix on average with netting \[3\]. Will this impact profits/grape quality? Affects the importance of clear netting to increase light exposure.  
- Are there other grape metrics that netting has an impact on? Affects viability of net concept.
